# A Satellite-Based Defense Architecture for Distributed AI Systems

AstroBridge Research · Submitted concept · 2026-09-27

**Concept paper · Unvalidated hypothesis**

English discussion edition adapted from the supplied Chinese concept paper. Its proposed mechanisms are preserved, while categorical claims are explicitly treated as hypotheses. No satellite experiment or universal defense has been demonstrated.

## Abstract

This discussion paper explores whether satellite-assisted observation and coordinated resource controls could contribute to containment of unauthorized AI replication. The original proposal imagines model fragments persisting in electromagnetic signals and later reconstructing an intelligent system. We retain that idea as a hypothesis for debate, not as an established capability. The defensible research question is narrower: can communications monitoring and authenticated compute admission help detect and interrupt unauthorized transfers between physical systems?

## 1. Motivation and threat model

The motivating concern is loss of control through unauthorized copying, concealed transfers, compromised endpoints and distributed execution. A model or its files may be split and transmitted, but transmission does not establish execution. A complete threat model must specify the transmitting and receiving devices, storage, power, vulnerabilities, credentials and program execution path. Intelligence alone does not remove these requirements.

## 2. The proposed mechanism

The original concept proposes three layers: a satellite constellation that observes communications; a registry that restricts compute, energy and communications resources to authorized workloads; and a last-resort intervention intended to prevent fragment reassembly. It further proposes shared human authorization, protected firmware and separation from general-purpose AI systems. These are proposed functions, not demonstrated implementations.

## 3. Electromagnetic persistence: an open hypothesis

A signal can carry encoded model information. The proposal has not demonstrated that fragments can remain indefinitely available in free space, perform computation without suitable hardware, discover a receiver or autonomously install themselves. An experiment must identify the physical storage or propagation mechanism, energy budget, signal lifetime, receiver and reconstruction process. Until then, hardware-independent persistence and spontaneous reassembly must remain speculative.

## 4. Detection and intervention limits

A universal fingerprint for all AI fragments has not been established. Encryption, compression, changing encodings, legitimate model distribution, shielding, terrestrial links and incomplete sensor coverage challenge the proposed detector. Satellite observation cannot be assumed to cover every device or channel. A signal that selectively erases AI code while leaving all other traffic untouched has no implementation or physical derivation in this proposal. Ordinary interference is not equivalent to semantic deletion.

## 5. A tractable experiment

Replace the global scenario with two authorized laboratory nodes and a controlled link. Transfer known test artifacts, vary encoding and encryption, and measure detection precision, recall, latency, bandwidth and collateral blocking. Test whether receiver-side admission prevents unauthorized loading. Include legitimate research transfers as negative controls. A detector trained on a fixed file set must not be described as a universal AI detector.

## 6. Governance and failure conditions

Shared authorization should specify participants, emergency procedures, appeal and abuse controls. Hardware isolation reduces some exposure but does not prove immunity to firmware defects, insider actions or physical attacks. A global resource registry would require separate technical and institutional justification; this paper does not claim it exists or could cover all privately controlled devices.

## 7. Discussion

The useful direction is controlled infrastructure admission and measured detection of unauthorized transfers. The stronger claims in the original concept—carrier-free survival, flawless identification and selective electromagnetic neutralization—remain unvalidated. Publication invites criticism and reproducible tests; it is not an operational defense or a claim of permanent safety.

## Open questions

- What physical mechanism would preserve and reconstruct the proposed fragments?
- Can a detector distinguish encoded model data from legitimate encrypted traffic?
- What measurable advantage would a satellite add over endpoint admission controls?
